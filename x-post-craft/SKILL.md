# X post craft

Based on Eliot's guide [The Mechanics of Attention](https://x.com/CDTEliot/status/2123493376528175223). Use it to draft new posts, punch up a rough idea, or review a draft before it goes out.

## 1. Gather inputs
- The idea, link, clip, article, or draft the user has.
- Who it's for (founders, designers, engineers, and so on) and what the user wants from it (reach, followers, discussion, traffic).
- If it's reacting to something, read the original post or conversation first (use the X tools for public posts).

## 2. Pass the party test
X is a party: don't spend the night talking about yourself. Before writing, answer:
- Why would anyone else want to engage with this?
- Is there something in it for the audience, or is it just about the user?
If it's pure self-promotion, reframe it around a lesson, a number, a story others can relate to, or a question. The goal is to give people something worth stopping for.

## 3. Pick an angle (use one or more)
- **One level deeper.** Plug into what everyone's already talking about and add the next step: take the joke further, find the chart behind the stat and explain it, find the real footage, dig up the salary band. Let the existing conversation carry half the distribution.
- **Deny an assumption.** Interesting ideas deny something the audience believes; boring ones affirm it (Murray Davis, "That's Interesting!", 1971). Write down "what most people think", then the claim that overturns it. Stay on the right side of the line: real tension, not contrarian for its own sake.
- **Remove friction.** Do the work for the reader. Turn a two-hour podcast into a short native clip with the key points summarized, or screenshot and highlight the exact passage from an article instead of just co-signing a link. Upload video natively, add chapters and timestamps for long content.

## 4. Pull at least one psychological trigger
1. **Identity validation**: put words to something readers think but never said ("that's exactly me").
2. **Status signaling**: give people a post whose sharing says something good about them.
3. **Tribal belonging**: draw a clear line around who it's for ("This is for founders who...").
4. **Productive discomfort**: show a gap between what people do and how smart they think they are, so they reply, defend, or bookmark.
5. **Aspiration**: show a result they want as reachable, with realistic timeframes, accessible methods, and specific numbers as proof.

## 5. Make it understandable in five seconds
- The first line says exactly what the reader gets.
- Key points are skimmable.
- Every bit of friction costs a like.

## 6. Make it visual
Text alone rarely stops the scroll. Pair the post with an image, chart, screenshot, short clip, or meme that makes the point in seconds (the most telling image from an article, a visual explainer for a confusing list, the video that captures what everyone's thinking).

## 7. For clips and quotes, choose moments that are
- contrarian takes that challenge conventional wisdom,
- specific, testable predictions,
- "deleted work" moments showing what people no longer need to do,
- concrete proof with numbers or outcomes,
- clear explanations of something complex in under 30 seconds.

## 8. Run the MATT test before posting
- **Memeable**: easy to remember, remix, or quote?
- **Additive**: adds something, or repeats what everyone knows?
- **True**: accurate and grounded?
- **Talkable**: will it naturally start conversations or get shared?
It should pass all four. If one is missing, rework it or don't post it.

## 9. Deliver
- Give 2 or 3 versions with different angles or triggers, each with a suggested visual, and say in one line which angle and trigger each uses and how it scores on MATT.
- When reviewing a draft, list what fails (party test, five-second test, MATT) and give a rewritten version.
- Never post for the user. Hand the final text over as a draft (use the X draft tools when available) so they press post.

## Ongoing habit
To keep ideas flowing, suggest the user curate their feed: follow accounts that surface good conversations, podcasts, and articles to react to, and unfollow ones that don't.
